# The Baker of Pudding Lane — voiceover script

149 words · about 42–46 s · one narrator, second person · captions on

## Facts used (checked; see sources at the end)
- The fire started about 1 a.m. on Sunday 2 September 1666 in Thomas Farriner's bakery, Pudding Lane, and burned until 6 September.
- Farriner said he had raked out his oven before bed, and insisted for the rest of his life that it was not the cause.
- 13,200 houses and 87 churches were destroyed (Museum of London figures).
- Robert Hubert, a French watchmaker from Rouen, confessed. He first named the wrong place (Westminster), then changed his story. He was hanged at Tyburn on 27 October 1666.
- Years later, the captain of the Swedish ship that brought him testified that Hubert did not reach London until two days after the fire started.
- Left out on purpose: the death toll (6 recorded, sources disagree), the wind, "the King's baker", St Paul's. All are commonly told but not checked for this script.

## Clean text for ElevenLabs (paste as one block)

You just burned down London. Or did you?
You're Thomas Farriner. A baker. Pudding Lane. One in the morning. September second. Sixteen sixty-six.
Before bed, you raked out your oven. Like every night.
You wake up to smoke. Your bakery is on fire.
It jumps to the next house. Then the next street. It burns for four days.
Thirteen thousand, two hundred houses. Gone. Eighty-seven churches. Gone.
And all of London is looking at your oven.
Then a stranger confesses. Robert Hubert. A French watchmaker. He even gets the place wrong. They hang him anyway.
Years later, a ship's captain speaks up. Hubert didn't reach London until two days after the fire started.
So the watchmaker didn't do it. And you swear your oven was out. Until the day you die.
So who started it? Comment B for the baker. Or a question mark if it's still a mystery.

## Delivery notes
- One steady tone from start to end. Tension rises through speed and shorter sentences, not volume or drama.
- Hook: flat and a little amused, like stating something obvious.
- Setting: quick list rhythm, a tiny lift on "Sixteen sixty-six."
- Lines 3–4: calm, then a slight push on "on fire."
- "Gone." each time: a short beat before it (about 0.3 s), said flat and low.
- "They hang him anyway.": deadpan, no sympathy in the voice.
- Reversal: slightly faster, like a revelation.
- Ending: dry, like a dare. Small pause before "So who started it?" No pause after the last word; the video ends on it.
- Almost no breaths or gaps; total silence between words under 1 s.
